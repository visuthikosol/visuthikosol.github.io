I'm an engineer who solves problems the way a designer sees them and a strategist evaluates them.

I studied Manufacturing and Design Engineering at Northwestern. On the design side, I built things for real people, like a one-handed PS4 controller that let a stroke survivor play FIFA again. On the process side, I worked on the systems behind the product, like validating a 3D motion-capture system for Shriners Children's Hospital. Along the way I founded AROThailand, a robotics education nonprofit, and moved from prototyping products to analyzing the numbers behind business decisions at Looloo Technology.

That work pushed me toward business school. I kept building things that worked technically, then realized I wanted to understand the why behind them and what happens after they're built, since that's usually where things stall. I came to Kellogg to build the business skills to answer bigger questions about how ideas actually get implemented and scaled inside organizations.

Outside of work, you'll usually find me bouldering, playing pickup soccer, or on the squash court.
