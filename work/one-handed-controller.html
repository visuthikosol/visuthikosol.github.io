Shirley Ryan AbilityLab

One-Handed PS4 Controller

The problemHector wanted to play his favorite game, FIFA, again. After a stroke, he only had one working hand.

Scroll ↓
The solution

A clamp-on lever system that brings every right-side button to his left hand, held steady on an adjustable stand.

01

The user

  • After his stroke, Hector has limited motor control in his right arm and leg, so he plays with his left hand only.
  • He can't reach all the buttons and triggers he needs in FIFA, which hurts his gameplay and is frustrating.
  • Goal: get him back to his previous level of play and enjoyment.
02

Final design

Levers on an axis of rotation, held by a clamp, let Hector press the right face buttons with his left hand like extra triggers. A weighted stand holds everything steady.

  1. Clamp and band
  2. Accessibility levers
  3. Stand
2.1

Clamp and band

  • 3D printed in PLA. Holds the levers on their axis so they don't slip off the face buttons.
  • Fits between the triggers and joysticks without blocking any front buttons.
  • A rod through the center hole is the axis, chosen for smoothness, durability, and a comfortable position found in user testing.
2.2

Accessibility levers

  • Let Hector use more fingers on his left hand, including sprint mapped to L2.
  • Four levers sit behind the controller. Pulling toward him presses the right face buttons, validated in user testing.
  • PLA for strength and cost, with fillets and wider ends for durability and consistent presses.
2.3

Stand

  • A short, weighted mic stand suited to how Hector grips the controller, for comfort over long sessions.
  • The 1.02 lb weighted base keeps it stable during play.
  • 180 degree rotation and adjustable height, added from user testing feedback.
