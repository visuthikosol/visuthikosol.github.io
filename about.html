I'm Tanik (Nick) Visuthikosol. I studied Manufacturing & Design Engineering at Northwestern's McCormick School of Engineering (Class of 2026, minor in ML & Data Science), and I'm now heading into the Kellogg School of Management's Master in Management program (Class of 2027).

I'm an engineer who solves problems the way a designer sees them and a strategist evaluates them.

My work spans human-centered design and process/manufacturing thinking. On the design side, I've built solutions grounded in direct user need: a one-handed PS4 controller extension at Shirley Ryan AbilityLab that let a stroke survivor play FIFA with his left hand again, and a low-cost arm rehabilitation machine at Chulalongkorn University's AI Lab. On the process side, I've worked on the systems behind the product — validating 3D motion-capture calibration tools for Shriners Children's Hospital through the Segal Design Institute, and reworking laundry pod dissolution mechanics from the ground up with WhiffleWash.

The throughline is always the same: understand the real problem, use data to test my assumptions, and land on a solution that works for the people and the business behind it. That data instinct carried into roles at Krungsri Consumer and Looloo Technology, where I moved from prototyping physical products to analyzing the numbers behind business decisions. I've also built that muscle from the other direction — founding AROThailand, a robotics education nonprofit, which meant thinking about funding, operations, and impact, not just design.

Outside of all that, you'll usually find me on a bouldering wall, in a pickup soccer game, or competing for a squash club team.
