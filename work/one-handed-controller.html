Shirley Ryan AbilityLab

One-Handed PS4 Controller

The problem

Hector wanted to play his favorite game, FIFA, again. After a stroke, he only had one working hand.

The solution

A clamp-on lever system that brings every right-side button to his left hand, held steady on an adjustable stand.

01

The user

  • After his stroke, Hector has limited motor control in his right arm and leg, so he plays with his left hand only.
  • He can't reach all the buttons and triggers he needs in FIFA, which hurts his gameplay and is frustrating.
  • Goal: get him back to his previous level of play and enjoyment.
02

Final design

Levers on an axis of rotation, held by a clamp, let Hector press the right face buttons with his left hand like extra triggers. A weighted stand holds everything steady.

  1. Clamp and band
  2. Accessibility levers
  3. Stand
2.1

Clamp and band

  • 3D printed PLA clamp holds the levers on their axis.
  • Fits between triggers and joysticks without blocking a button.
  • A center rod acts as the axis, tuned in user testing.
2.2

Accessibility levers

  • Four levers behind the controller press the right face buttons.
  • Sprint moves to L2, so more fingers stay in play.
  • Filleted, wider ends for strength and consistent presses.
2.3

Stand

  • Short, weighted mic stand matched to his grip.
  • 1.02 lb base keeps it steady mid-game.
  • 180 degree rotation and adjustable height from testing feedback.
